Walkthrough & Guide

Everything you need to conquer Shandalar.

Guide by VigilantSera, world co-designer. Prefer reading on Google Docs? Open the doc.

Getting Started

You start in the map's center in the wastes biome. One thing to keep in mind is that the game is very open-ended. As such, you may run into tough enemies early. In the early game, you'll want to avoid enemies in dungeons and take the loot they guard without fighting them whenever possible.

Your first priority should be collecting a full set of equipment. There are five equipment slots: head/necklace, torso, left arm, right arm, and boots. Equipment can be found in shops, dungeons, from bosses, and from mini-bosses. Finding the right equipment can be as important as building the right deck, but at first, you'll want to fill up all your available slots if able.

Many wastes towns sell cheap defensive equipment (in the store marked with a sword) that can be great early game pickups to bolster your low starting life total. Also keep an eye out for equipment that gives starting creatures — these can be excellent early before you've found stronger options.

Focusing on quests early is not recommended — the quests are randomized and may lead you to visit a dungeon or fight enemies you're not ready for. Waiting until you've improved your deck, bolstered your starting life, and gotten some equipment before diving into questing is highly recommended.

Also note that while the game has a "main quest" it is strictly optional and only guides you to locations you could go anyway. No content is gated behind the main quest.

Your first exploration goal should be to visit each of the capitol cities in the center of each of the colored biomes. These offer: life increases, teleport runes, unique card shops, and arenas with equipment rewards.

Most enemies and regions are more likely to give cards of their color, so if you are focusing on one or two colors early (recommended), heading to that region first will be highly beneficial.

You begin the game with a gold coin that provides a free draft. When you draft, you receive one pack for one win, two packs plus a new coin for two wins, and two packs, a coin, and the drafted deck for a perfect three-win run.

Old school drafts can be tricky! Fliers and pingers are invaluable as is of course removal, and don't underestimate mid-sized creatures even if they seem inefficient on mana.

Each color starts with a different pre-constructed deck — there are 113 in total across 27 editions, from Fourth Edition through Scourge. Green and White are generally more forgiving for beginners.

Standout Cards to Look Out For

Veterans of older magic may know most of these, but here are some recommended cards to look out for, split into common and uncommon ‘staples’ and broadly or especially powerful rares.

Also keep an eye open for color hate cards – the game doesn’t support side-boarding but since you will almost always know what kind of opponent you’re facing, putting appropriate color hate against a foe can often make a huge difference!

Endgame: The Color Castles (5 bosses)

Each color castle has five ‘guard’ NPCs, you must defeat at least four to reach the boss. Defeating the boss grants a key. Once you have all five keys you may unlock one of the gates behind the boss (but just one) to get that color Mox plus the strange key that opens the temple of the false god.

Endgame: Temple of the False God (26 bosses)

Once you have obtained the strange key from defeating all five color palaces, you are ready for the final challenges. This unlocks the gate into the temple of the false god (located in the wastes area and marked by a palace icon on the map).

Inside, there are five portals to five more palaces, even more challenging than the previous ones. Defeat all five to access Karona the False God herself. These palaces all have lots of starting cards, including color hate for the palace’s two enemy colors.

Quick Tips for New Players

Explore Every Town

Towns sell cards and offer quests. Visit shops frequently and buy cards that strengthen your deck's strategy rather than just adding powerful cards.

Save Before Bosses

Boss fights are significantly harder than regular encounters. Save your game before entering any dungeon or lair so you can retry with a different strategy.

Manage Your Deck Size

Keep your deck close to 60 cards. A bloated deck dilutes your key cards. Sell extra cards at towns for gold to buy what you actually need.

Items Are Powerful

Equipment items provide permanent bonuses like extra life, starting cards in play, or mana advantages. Prioritize item-granting encounters.

Try Commander Mode

Once you've completed a standard run, try Commander Mode for a wildly different experience. You build around a legendary creature from the old-border era.

Loot System

How card rewards work in Old Border Shandalar

Card Tiers

Every card in the game is ranked into one of six quality tiers based on its real-world value and competitive playability. You can browse the full tier lists in this Google Sheet. The pool contains 6,242 unique cards across the entire old-border era (1993–2003). The Power 9 (Black Lotus, Ancestral Recall, etc.) are not part of the regular drop system, but they can still be obtained through other means in the game.

Enemy Drops

Stronger enemies have better odds of dropping high-tier cards. The system works like a Diablo-style loot curve — every enemy can drop any tier, but the odds scale with their strength.

Enemy TierAvg CardsWhat to Expect
Elite Boss~10Guaranteed S-tier, excellent A-tier odds
Major Boss~10High S-tier chance, reliable A and B drops
Boss~8Decent S-tier chance, mostly A and B drops
Mini-Boss~7Small S-tier chance, mostly B and C drops
Hard~5Rare high-tier drops, mostly C and D
Medium~3Mostly D and E tier
Easy~3Almost always bulk (D and E)

What can drop: Enemies can reward you with three types of card drops:

  • Tiered reward cards — drawn from the tier pool, weighted by enemy strength
  • Deck cards — cards from the enemy's own deck, so you can steal their best tricks
  • Hand-picked cards — specific cards placed by the designers as unique rewards

Color matching: Tiered card drops are weighted toward the enemy's colors. A red mage mostly drops red cards, with a smaller chance of colorless artifacts and lands.

Boss rewards: Bosses are best-of-3 matches, so their better drop rates reflect the extra effort. Many bosses also split their hand-picked card rewards with a nearby treasure chest, so be sure to explore after a boss fight!

Difficulty scaling: Higher difficulty settings reduce the bonus cards per drop. On Insane, you get the base amount with no bonus. On Easy, you get up to 50% extra.

Treasure Chests

Treasure chests in dungeons use the same tier system as enemies. Each chest's drop quality is based on the strongest enemy nearby — chests in a boss room drop better cards than chests in an early dungeon.

Unlike enemy drops, chests are not color-filtered — you can find cards of any color. This makes chests a great way to pick up off-color staples for splashing or building a new deck.

Many chests also contain hand-picked items and cards placed by the designers in addition to the tiered rewards.

There are 552 treasure chests spread across 274 dungeon maps.

Shops

Town shops also stock cards using the tier system, but with much lower odds of rare cards appearing. Each shop visit, the inventory is rolled fresh.

What you'll typically see: Mostly C and D tier cards, with a small chance of B-tier and a rare chance of A or S-tier cards appearing. There's roughly a 1-in-25 chance of an S-tier card showing up per shop page.

Shops respect their theme — a Goblin shop only stocks Goblin cards, a red city shop only stocks red cards, and land shops only sell lands.

Tip: Broad shops like the general "Random" shop have the best odds of stocking rare finds, while narrow tribal shops may rarely show their best cards.
