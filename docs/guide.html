★ Official Walkthrough & Guide ★
Guide by VigilantSera, world co-designer. Prefer Google Docs? Read it there.

Getting Started

You start in the map's center in the wastes biome. The game is very open-ended — you may run into tough enemies early. In the early game, avoid enemies in dungeons and take the loot they guard without fighting them whenever possible.

Your first priority should be collecting a full set of equipment. There are five equipment slots: head/necklace, torso, left arm, right arm, and boots. Equipment can be found in shops, dungeons, from bosses, and from mini-bosses.

Many wastes towns sell cheap defensive equipment that can bolster your low starting life total. Also keep an eye out for equipment that gives starting creatures.

Focusing on quests early is not recommended — the quests are randomized and may lead you to difficult encounters. Waiting until you've improved your deck and equipment is highly recommended.

The "main quest" is strictly optional and does not gate any content.

Visit each capitol city in the center of each colored biome for: life increases, teleport runes, unique card shops, and arenas with equipment rewards.

Most enemies and regions give cards of their color, so focus on one or two colors early (recommended) and head to that region first.

You begin with a gold coin for a free draft. One win = one pack. Two wins = two packs + coin. Three wins = two packs, coin, and the drafted deck.

Old school drafts can be tricky! Fliers and pingers are invaluable, as is removal. Don't underestimate mid-sized creatures.

Each color starts with a different pre-constructed deck — there are 113 in total across 27 editions, from Fourth Edition through Scourge. Green and White are generally more forgiving for beginners.

Standout Cards to Look Out For
Veterans of older magic may know most of these, but here are some recommended cards to look out for, split into common and uncommon ‘staples’ and broadly or especially powerful rares.

Also keep an eye open for color hate cards – the game doesn’t support side-boarding but since you will almost always know what kind of opponent you’re facing, putting appropriate color hate against a foe can often make a huge difference!

Endgame: The Color Castles (5 bosses)
Each color castle has five ‘guard’ NPCs, you must defeat at least four to reach the boss. Defeating the boss grants a key. Once you have all five keys you may unlock one of the gates behind the boss (but just one) to get that color Mox plus the strange key that opens the temple of the false god.

Endgame: Temple of the False God (26 bosses)
Once you have obtained the strange key from defeating all five color palaces, you are ready for the final challenges. This unlocks the gate into the temple of the false god (located in the wastes area and marked by a palace icon on the map). Inside, there are five portals to five more palaces, even more challenging than the previous ones. Defeat all five to access Karona the False God herself. These palaces all have lots of starting cards, including color hate for the palace’s two enemy colors.

★ Loot System ★
How card rewards work in Old Border Shandalar

★ Card Tiers
Every card in the game is ranked into one of six quality tiers. Browse the full tier lists in this Google Sheet.

Tier Cards Examples
S49Mox Diamond, Sol Ring, Necropotence, Yawgmoth's Will, Channel, Mishra's Workshop
A246Force of Will, Demonic Tutor, Tolarian Academy, Library of Alexandria, Survival of the Fittest
B948Counterspell, Lightning Bolt, Swords to Plowshares, Brainstorm, Natural Order
C1,844Wrath of God, Dark Ritual, Birds of Paradise, Reanimate, Rhystic Study
D2,395Duress, Price of Progress, Careful Study, Moment's Peace
E760Gray Ogre, Storm Crow, Goblin Piker, Carnival of Souls

The tier pool contains 6,242 unique cards across the entire old-border era (1993–2003).

Note: The Power 9 are not part of the regular drop system, but can still be obtained through other means.



★ Enemy Drops
Stronger enemies have better odds of dropping high-tier cards. The system works like a Diablo-style loot curve.

Enemy Tier Avg Cards What to Expect
Elite Boss~10Guaranteed S-tier, excellent A-tier odds
Major Boss~10High S-tier chance, reliable A and B drops
Boss~8Decent S-tier chance, mostly A and B drops
Mini-Boss~7Small S-tier chance, mostly B and C drops
Hard~5Rare high-tier drops, mostly C and D
Medium~3Mostly D and E tier
Easy~3Almost always bulk (D and E)

What can drop:
 • Tiered reward cards — drawn from the tier pool, weighted by enemy strength
 • Deck cards — cards from the enemy's own deck
 • Hand-picked cards — specific cards placed by the designers

Color matching: Tiered drops are weighted toward the enemy's colors.

Boss rewards: Bosses are best-of-3. Many bosses split hand-picked rewards with a nearby treasure chest.

Difficulty scaling: Higher difficulty = fewer bonus cards. Insane = base amount only. Easy = up to 50% extra.



★ Treasure Chests
Chests use the same tier system. Drop quality is based on the strongest nearby enemy.

Chests are not color-filtered — great for off-color staples.

Many contain hand-picked items and cards in addition to tiered rewards.

552 treasure chests across 274 dungeon maps.



★ Shops
Shops stock cards using the tier system with lower rare odds. Inventory rerolls each visit.

Mostly C and D tier, with a 1-in-25 chance of S-tier per page.

Shops respect their theme. Broad shops like "Random" have the best odds of rare finds.
