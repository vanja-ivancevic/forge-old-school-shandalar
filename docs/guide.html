★ Loot System ★
How card rewards work in Old Border Shandalar

★ Card Tiers
Every card in the game is ranked into one of six quality tiers based on its real-world value and competitive playability. You can browse the full tier lists in this Google Sheet.

Tier Cards Examples
S 49 Mox Diamond, Sol Ring, Necropotence, Yawgmoth's Will, Channel, Mishra's Workshop
A 246 Force of Will, Demonic Tutor, Tolarian Academy, Library of Alexandria, Survival of the Fittest
B 948 Counterspell, Lightning Bolt, Swords to Plowshares, Brainstorm, Natural Order

D 2,395 Duress, Price of Progress, Careful Study, Moment's Peace

The tier pool contains 6,242 unique cards across the entire old-border era (1993–2003).

Note: The Power 9 (Black Lotus, Ancestral Recall, etc.) are not part of the regular drop system, but they can still be obtained through other means in the game.



★ Enemy Drops
Stronger enemies have better odds of dropping high-tier cards. The system works like a Diablo-style loot curve — every enemy can drop any tier, but the odds scale with their strength.

Enemies are classified into strength tiers based on their difficulty:

Enemy Tier Avg Cards What to Expect
Elite Boss ~10 Guaranteed S-tier, excellent A-tier odds
Major Boss ~10 High S-tier chance, reliable A and B drops
Boss ~8 Decent S-tier chance, mostly A and B drops
Mini-Boss ~7 Small S-tier chance, mostly B and C drops
Hard ~5 Rare high-tier drops, mostly C and D
Medium ~3 Mostly D and E tier
Easy ~3 Almost always bulk (D and E)

What can drop: Enemies can reward you with three types of card drops:
  • Tiered reward cards — drawn from the tier pool, weighted by enemy strength (see table above)
  • Deck cards — cards from the enemy's own deck, so you can steal their best tricks
  • Hand-picked cards — specific cards placed by the designers as unique rewards for certain enemies

Color matching: Tiered card drops are weighted toward the enemy's colors. A red mage mostly drops red cards, with a smaller chance of colorless artifacts and lands.

Boss rewards: Bosses are best-of-3 matches, so their better drop rates reflect the extra effort. Many bosses also split their hand-picked card rewards with a nearby treasure chest, so be sure to explore after a boss fight!

Difficulty scaling: Higher difficulty settings reduce the bonus cards per drop. On Insane, you get the base amount with no bonus. On Easy, you get up to 50% extra.



★ Treasure Chests
Treasure chests in dungeons use the same tier system as enemies. Each chest's drop quality is based on the strongest enemy nearby — chests in a boss room drop better cards than chests in an early dungeon.

Unlike enemy drops, chests are not color-filtered — you can find cards of any color. This makes chests a great way to pick up off-color staples for splashing or building a new deck.

Many chests also contain hand-picked items and cards placed by the designers in addition to the tiered rewards.

There are 552 treasure chests spread across 274 dungeon maps.



★ Shops
Town shops also stock cards using the tier system, but with much lower odds of rare cards appearing. Each shop visit, the inventory is rolled fresh.

What you'll typically see: Mostly C and D tier cards, with a small chance of B-tier and a rare chance of A or S-tier cards appearing. There's roughly a 1-in-25 chance of an S-tier card showing up per shop page.

Shops respect their theme — a Goblin shop only stocks Goblin cards, a red city shop only stocks red cards, and land shops only sell lands. The tier system works within these filters, so specialized shops may have fewer high-tier options.

Tip: Broad shops like the general "Random" shop have the best odds of stocking rare finds, while narrow tribal shops may rarely show their best cards.



★ Gold & Shards
Enemies also drop gold and mana shards, scaled to their strength:

Enemy Tier Gold Shards
Elite Boss 10,000–15,000 300–400
Major Boss 3,000–5,000 80–120
Boss 800–1,200 20–30
Mini-Boss 150–300 5–10
Hard 50–150 2–5
Medium 20–80 1–3
Easy 5–30 0–1

Bosses always pay out gold and shards. Lower enemies have a chance of dropping nothing — you won't get rich grinding easy fights.
